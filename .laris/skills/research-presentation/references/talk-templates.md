# Talk templates

## Timing rule of thumb
About 1 slide per minute for technical talks; fewer for math-heavy slides. Reserve time for Q&A.

## 15-minute conference talk (example)
| # | Slide | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Title | 0.5 |
| 2 | Problem and motivation | 1.5 |
| 3 | What exists and the gap | 1.5 |
| 4 | Contribution (3 bullets max) | 1 |
| 5-7 | Method (model, controller, key idea) | 4 |
| 8-10 | Results (main comparison, ablation/sensitivity, failure case) | 4 |
| 11 | Limitations and future work | 1 |
| 12 | Takeaway | 1 |
| - | Backup slides | - |

## Thesis defense (example structure, adapt to the university's rules)
Motivation -> objectives and RQs -> literature and gap -> contribution per chapter (problem, method,
result, limitation) -> synthesis -> conclusions and future work -> publications list -> backups.

## Slide design
Large fonts; figures over text; one equation per slide when possible with symbols explained;
consistent colors per method across slides; slide numbers.

## Rehearsal checklist
Full run timed twice; transitions between sections; backup slides indexed; answers to top questions
rehearsed; equipment and file formats checked (PDF fallback).
